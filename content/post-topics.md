# Ryan Provost Editorial Roadmap

This roadmap turns Ryan's career evidence into a durable body of thought leadership. The goal is to demonstrate how he thinks as an operator: specific, technically credible, commercially aware, and calm under pressure. Posts should teach from experience without exposing confidential client information or reading like disguised case studies.

## Brand guardrails

- Write for technology executives, operating leaders, transformation sponsors, board members, and senior practitioners.
- Start with a real decision, failure mode, or operating tension. Avoid generic leadership advice.
- Use concrete examples and measures where they are approved for public use. Generalize client details by default.
- Connect strategy to implementation. Every post should leave the reader with a clearer decision or a practical way to work.
- Keep the tone direct and generous. Do not posture, oversell, or manufacture certainty.
- Favor 800 to 1,200 words for primary essays and 300 to 600 words for field notes.

## Topic backlog

### Enterprise transformation and operating models

1. **The assessment is not the transformation**  
   A diagnostic creates options; accountable ownership, funding, sequencing, and governance create change.

2. **Turning 275 procedures into a portfolio leaders can fund**  
   How to move from process inventory to a prioritized set of transformation opportunities without losing evidence.

3. **Why transformation programs stall between contract and delivery**  
   The gap between commercial intent and executable work: scope, dependencies, access, owners, and decision rights.

4. **A PMO should improve decisions, not decorate status**  
   What useful program governance makes visible and why more reporting rarely solves weak ownership.

5. **The hidden cost of unowned dependencies**  
   How infrastructure, data, security, access, and client decisions quietly become the critical path.

6. **How to build a RAID practice executives will actually use**  
   Replace passive registers with evidence, consequences, accountable owners, required decisions, and dates.

7. **When a portfolio needs one operating model and when it does not**  
   Standardize the decisions and evidence that matter while leaving delivery teams room to work.

### Platform architecture and data

8. **Kafka can move the data. It cannot make the data agree.**  
   Data rationalization starts upstream. Definitions, ownership, canonical models, and event contracts shape every workflow and downstream decision.

9. **When custom Excel workbooks become systems of record**  
   Why spreadsheets persist, what they reveal, and how to modernize without dismissing the knowledge they contain.

10. **Modernization without the big-bang rewrite**  
    How to isolate value, sequence risk, and create reusable seams around a legacy platform.

11. **Design reusable capabilities, not a collection of apps**  
    The architectural and economic difference between solving one launch and changing the cost of every future launch.

12. **Architecture reviews should end in decisions**  
    A practical structure for turning diagrams and technical findings into choices, owners, and next steps.

13. **APIs are organizational contracts**  
    Interfaces clarify ownership and change boundaries as much as they move data.

14. **Build versus buy is a portfolio decision**  
    Evaluate fit, control, integration, operating cost, switching cost, and time to learning—not feature lists alone.

15. **Data rationalization has an upstream and downstream blast radius**  
    Trace how one contradictory definition moves through Kafka topics, APIs, warehouses, operational workflows, reporting, and executive decisions.

### Delivery recovery and engineering organizations

16. **Evidence before green status**  
    Why response payloads, test results, deployment artifacts, and working software matter more than optimistic reporting.

17. **Recovering a program without replacing everyone**  
    Restore clarity, engineering discipline, and trust before assuming the people are the problem.

18. **What 80 percent test coverage changed and what it did not**  
    Testing can restore confidence and speed, but it cannot replace product decisions or coherent architecture.

19. **Scaling from one team to a portfolio without losing accountability**  
    The operating rhythms, leadership layers, and decision boundaries that keep scale from becoming diffusion.

20. **Good engineers need business context**  
    Better technical judgment follows when teams understand the customer, economics, constraints, and consequences.

21. **The executive sponsor's job when delivery turns red**  
    Create decision speed, protect truth, remove ambiguity, and stop rewarding performative confidence.

22. **Leading distributed teams across time zones and company lines**  
    Make ownership, handoffs, evidence, and escalation explicit when proximity cannot do the work for you.

### Product experience and portfolio economics

23. **The business case is part of the product**  
    Investment logic belongs beside experience, architecture, and delivery—not after them.

24. **Experience strategy that survives implementation**  
    Connect personas and journeys to capabilities, data, integration, feasibility, and a funded roadmap.

25. **Turning personas into funded capabilities**  
    How to preserve human needs while translating design research into executable enterprise choices.

26. **Time to learning matters more than feature count**  
    Use smaller launches and better feedback to reduce uncertainty before scaling investment.

27. **Platform economics for product leaders**  
    Make shared infrastructure, capacity, reuse, launch cost, and portfolio value visible in prioritization.

28. **When faster launch time changes portfolio strategy**  
    Reducing a launch cycle from months to days changes which experiments and business models become viable.

### Executive leadership and sustainable performance

29. **Calm is a leadership capability**  
    Pressure reveals the habits, trust, and operating discipline built before the crisis.

30. **Sustainable performance is an operating discipline**  
    Boundaries, recovery, and health shape judgment, retention, and execution over years—not just individual wellbeing.

31. **Coaching technical leaders who now lead leaders**  
    The transition from having answers to creating clarity, standards, and better decisions through others.

32. **Staying technical as executive scope grows**  
    Maintain enough depth to test assumptions and earn trust without becoming the team's most expensive individual contributor.

33. **How to say no without stopping momentum**  
    Replace vague resistance with the constraint, tradeoff, decision owner, and viable next path.

### AI-enabled operations

34. **AI use cases need an operating model before a model**  
    Value depends on workflow ownership, evidence, controls, integration, and adoption as much as technical capability.

35. **How to evaluate AI value without inventing savings**  
    Separate capacity, avoidance, redeployment, quality, cycle time, revenue, and realized financial outcomes.

36. **Human accountability in agentic workflows**  
    Define decision rights, escalation, evidence, and control points before software acts across operational systems.

37. **Readiness before automation**  
    Weak data, unclear ownership, unstable processes, and missing controls do not disappear when AI is added.

## Suggested publishing rhythm

- Publish one primary essay every two weeks.
- Use the alternating weeks for a shorter field note, diagram, or response to a current industry development.
- Reuse each primary essay as a LinkedIn post, a short carousel outline, and two or three discussion prompts.
- Review the backlog quarterly so current work sharpens the writing without turning the site into a client diary.
